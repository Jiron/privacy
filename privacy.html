4. General principles

4.1 What data I process and where it comes from

  • Technical data that every request on the internet carries: your IP address, the date and time, the address you requested, and details your browser sends, such as its name and version and the page you came from. My own servers store only the small part of this that section 5.5 describes. Cloudflare and GitHub handle it on their systems in order to deliver the pages (sections 5.7 and 5.8).
  • What you put into an API request: the title and text, a username or display name, and the addresses of a profile picture and a background image (section 5.4).
  • Discord account data, only when you join my Discord server: your display name, your username, the address of your profile picture and your user ID, which the bot uses to mention you (section 5.6).
  • Contact data, only if you write to me: your email address and what you write (section 5.11).

Most of this comes from you. Two things usually do not. The names and pictures in an API request are normally sent by the operator of a bot and are about a member of their Discord server. And the welcome bot receives its data from Discord.

You do not have to give me any data. Without the technical data a page cannot be delivered, and without the values in a link the generator has nothing to draw.

I do not ask for and do not knowingly process: payment details, health data, biometric data, identity documents, the content of your Discord messages, or your location beyond what an IP address roughly shows. There are no user accounts.

Where the GDPR or the UK GDPR applies to you, these are the legal bases:

  • Providing what you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR): delivering a page you opened, and drawing an image you requested.

My interest is a free service that works and is safe. I weigh it against your interest in being left alone by keeping the data small and the storage short.

Nothing on these projects relies on your consent at the moment. If that ever changes, I will ask you first, and you will be able to withdraw your consent at any time.

The services are built to hold as little as possible: no accounts, no database, logs without full IP addresses and without the contents of a request. The generator refuses image links that point into private or internal networks.

No system on the internet is perfectly safe. If something ever goes wrong in a way that puts your data at high risk, I will tell the people affected and the authority, as the law requires.

4.7 Cookies and local storage

My pages and the API set no cookies and store nothing in your browser's local storage. There is no analytics, no advertising and no fingerprinting, and nothing is embedded from other companies.

One kind of cookie is outside my code. My domains are protected by Cloudflare, and when Cloudflare checks whether a visitor is a person or a bot, it may set a security cookie in your browser:

  • cf_clearance – remembers that your browser passed a check, so that you are not checked again on every page. Cloudflare sets its lifetime; it can be up to one year.
  • __cf_bm – helps Cloudflare tell people from bots. It lasts 30 minutes.

These cookies can be set for jiron.dev and discorddevtools.xyz, including their subdomains. So you may find a cf_clearance cookie for jiron.dev in your browser even though the homepage itself never sets one. They hold a random token, not your name. They are used for security only, not for advertising, and they do not follow you to other websites.

Because these cookies are strictly necessary for security, the law does not require me to ask for your consent, but it does require me to tell you about them, which this section does. You can delete or block them in your browser at any time. My pages keep working; Cloudflare may then simply check your browser again.

I do not knowingly process data of younger children. If you believe a child's data has reached me, write to me and I will delete it without delay.

4.9 Automated decisions

5.4 DiscordDevTools API and Welcome Image Generator

The API (https://api.discorddevtools.xyz) is free and needs no key. The Welcome Image Generator (https://api.discorddevtools.xyz/welcome-image-generator/generate.png) is driven entirely by the values in the link. Those values typically contain a username or display name, a title and a greeting text, and may contain the address of a profile picture and of a background image. This is personal data of the person the image is about, and that person is usually not you but a member of the Discord server the image is made for.

What happens with it:

  • The values are used to draw the image you asked for, and for nothing else.
  • If you pass the address of a picture, my server downloads the picture from that address so that it can be drawn into the image. The address must be public; links into private or internal networks are refused. A downloaded picture is held in memory for up to 10 minutes and is never written to disk.
  • Unless you add cacheImage=false, the finished image is stored in a cache, so that the same link does not have to be drawn again. The cache files the image under a hash of the values, not under the values themselves, and deletes it at the latest 7 days after it was drawn. With cacheImage=false the image is neither read from nor written to any cache.
  • The values in the link are not written to any log (section 5.5).
  • Once an image is shown in Discord, Discord and Cloudflare may keep their own cached copy under their own rules. I cannot delete those copies.

If you build on the API, you are responsible for the data you send. If you pass other people's personal data, such as the names and pictures of your server's members, you need your own legal basis for doing so and should tell your members about it. Pass only what the image needs. A link is not a secret: do not put anything into it that should not appear on the image, and never credentials or tokens.

To have a cached image removed before its 7 days are over, send me the link that produced it (chapter 6).

5.5 Logs and rate limits of the API

The API writes one line per request into a text file, and starts a new file every day. A line contains:

  • the date and time of the request;
  • your IP address in shortened form only: an IPv4 address is cut to its first three parts (a /24 network) and an IPv6 address to its first 48 bits (a /48 network), so that the line no longer points to a single connection;
  • the HTTP method, the requested path without the query string, the response status and how long the request took;
  • how the request was handled: the name of the image template, whether the image came from the cache, and whether a rate limit refused the request.

The query string, which is where names, texts and picture addresses are, is never written to these files. Neither are request headers, your browser's name, cookies or request bodies. The generator behind the API logs only the route, the response status and the duration of a request, with no address and no values at all.

These files are deleted after 14 days. Their purpose is to keep the service secure, to recognise attacks and abuse, and to find faults. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in a safe service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Even a shortened IP address with a time can, in rare cases, be connected to a person, so I treat these files as personal data.

To enforce the rate limits, the API counts recent requests per address in the memory of the server for a short rolling period, and remembers for a few minutes a hash of the image links it has just served. Neither is written to disk or to a log.

6. Your rights

6.1 What you can ask for

  • Access: to know whether I hold data about you, and to get a copy of it.
  • Correction: to have wrong data put right.
  • Deletion: to have your data deleted.
  • Restriction: to have the use of your data limited.
  • Objection: you can object at any time to processing that I base on my legitimate interests (section 4.2), for reasons that come from your own situation. I then stop, unless I have compelling reasons to continue or need the data for legal claims. The short security log is such a case as a rule, because the service cannot be run safely without it.
  • Portability: to receive data that you gave me yourself in a common file format.
  • Withdrawing consent: if I ever process data on the basis of your consent, you can withdraw it at any time for the future.

I may limit or refuse a request where the law allows or requires it, for example to protect another person, or where a law obliges me to keep the data.

6.2 How to ask

Write to contact@jiron.dev. A request costs nothing. I answer within 30 days. If a request is unusually complex I may need longer, and I will tell you so within those 30 days.

I do not ask for an identity document. If I cannot tell from your message that the data is about you, I may ask a question first. Please be aware that I keep very little: a shortened IP address cannot be traced to a person, so I often will not be able to find anything about you. It helps if you send what you have, for example the link you used with the generator, or the date and time of a request.

6.3 Complaints

You can complain to a supervisory authority. In Switzerland this is the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC): https://www.edoeb.admin.ch

If you live in the EU or the EEA, you can turn to the authority of the country where you live or work, or where you think the law was broken. A list is at https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/about-edpb/members_en. In the United Kingdom it is the Information Commissioner's Office (https://ico.org.uk). I would be glad if you wrote to me first, so that I can try to put things right.
